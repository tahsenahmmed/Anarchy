Cell Biology: The Hidden Machinery of Life

A clear introduction to cells, the basic structural and functional units of living organisms.

Anarchy Learning Notes · 7 min read

What is a cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Every organism is made of one or more cells. Some organisms, such as bacteria, consist of a single cell; plants and animals are multicellular and contain many specialized cell types.

Cells take in materials, obtain and use energy, maintain internal conditions, respond to their surroundings, and reproduce. In multicellular organisms, cells also communicate and cooperate to form tissues and organs.

Two broad cell types

Prokaryotic cells

Prokaryotes, including bacteria and archaea, do not enclose their DNA inside a membrane-bound nucleus. Their DNA is located in a region called the nucleoid. They also lack the membrane-bound organelles found in eukaryotic cells.

Eukaryotic cells

Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus and specialized compartments called organelles. Animals, plants, fungi, and protists are eukaryotes. Plant cells commonly have a cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues, and a large central vacuole.

Key cell structures

  • Nucleus: stores most of the cell's genetic information and helps regulate gene expression.
  • Mitochondria: generate much of the ATP used by eukaryotic cells through cellular respiration.
  • Ribosomes: build proteins by translating messenger RNA.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum: helps synthesize proteins and lipids.
  • Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts, and packages many proteins and lipids.
  • Lysosomes: contain enzymes that digest and recycle cellular material in many animal cells.

The cell membrane

The cell membrane forms a selective boundary between the cell and its environment. It consists mainly of a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. Some substances cross by diffusion, others use membrane proteins, and some require energy-dependent transport.

This selective permeability helps cells maintain suitable internal conditions, a process closely connected to homeostasis.

Quick revision

  • Cell = basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotes have one.
  • Ribosomes synthesize proteins.
  • Mitochondria produce much of the ATP in eukaryotic cells.
  • The cell membrane regulates exchange with the environment.
Key takeawayCells are the smallest units that can independently carry out the essential processes of life. Their structures are specialized, but they work together as an integrated system.
